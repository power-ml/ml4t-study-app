G1: Matching Models to Problem Characteristics 
[ML-D10G1Q1] 
A systematic equity team ﬁnds that next-day returns change approximately in proportion to a small set of standardized 
factors. The PM wants a dependable baseline that can be retrained frequently and asks whether the team should begin 
with a deep tree instead of a linear model simply because the tree is more ﬂexible. 
 
A. A linear model is a reasonable starting point when the relevant relationship is approximately linear. 
B. A more ﬂexible learner must have lower out-of-sample error because it can represent more functions. 
C. A simple model may outperform a complex model when the sample is limited or noisy. 
D. Comparing candidates on representative validation data is preferable to choosing solely from their theoretical 
ﬂexibility. 
E. Standardizing the factors automatically makes every relationship linear.

Answer Key: 
 
A. True – The model structure matches the observed proportional relationship and provides a useful baseline. 
B. False – Additional ﬂexibility can ﬁt noise and does not guarantee better generalization. 
C. True – Lower variance can make a simpler learner more reliable under limited information. 
D. True – Validation evidence tests how the tradeoff behaves on unseen observations. 
E. False – Scaling changes feature units, not the underlying functional form.
