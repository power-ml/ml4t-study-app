[ML-D5G1Q1] 
A researcher uses KNN to predict next-day return from two features: daily return expressed as a decimal, typically 
between -0.03 and 0.03, and trading volume measured in millions of shares, typically between 1 and 500. The features 
are used without scaling. The researcher notices that the selected neighbors have nearly identical volume but quite 
different recent returns. 
 
A. Under Euclidean distance, the volume feature is likely to dominate because its numerical range is much larger. 
B. Converting volume from shares to millions of shares can change which observations are nearest unless the features 
are scaled consistently. 
C. KNN automatically compensates for different feature units when calculating ordinary Euclidean distance. 
D. Standardizing both features using training-set statistics can make their contributions to distance more comparable. 
E. Feature scaling must be learned from the full dataset, including the test period, to produce unbiased distances. 
 
Answer Key: 
 
A. True – A feature with a much larger numerical range can dominate Euclidean distance. 
B. True – Changing one feature's units changes its contribution to distance and may change neighbor rankings. 
C. False – Ordinary Euclidean distance uses the supplied numerical values without automatic unit correction. 
D. True – Standardization can prevent raw scale alone from determining similarity. 
E. False – Scaling parameters should be estimated from training data to avoid test-data leakage.

A. Because Euclidean distance is calculated from absolute numerical differences, the much larger numerical scale of 
Market Capitalization can dominate the distance calculation and therefore determine most of the neighbor ordering. 
B. KNN is invariant to feature scaling because it depends only on the rank ordering of observations within each individual 
feature. 
C. Without feature scaling, variation in the Debt-to-Equity Ratio may contribute so little to the total distance that the 
model behaves almost as though Market Capitalization were its only meaningful feature. 
D. Replacing Euclidean distance with Manhattan distance would, by itself, eliminate the scale imbalance because

Answer Key: 
A. True – Euclidean distance between observations 𝑥and 𝑦is 
𝑑(𝑥,𝑦) = √((𝑥₁ − 𝑦₁)² + (𝑥₂ − 𝑦₂)²). 
 
The calculation uses differences in the original units of each feature. Because Market Capitalization varies by hundreds

dominate the distance. Neighbor selection will then be driven primarily by differences in Market Capitalization. (Ref: ISLR 
Ch. 2.2) 
B. False – KNN is highly sensitive to feature scaling because its predictions depend on distances calculated jointly across 
the features. Rescaling one feature can change the relative distances between observations, alter which observations 
are selected as neighbors, and ultimately change the predicted class.

C. True – The Debt-to-Equity Ratio is not literally removed from the distance calculation, but its numerical contribution 
can become negligible relative to that of Market Capitalization. In practical terms, the two-dimensional neighborhood 
may behave almost like a one-dimensional neighborhood determined by Market Capitalization. 
D. False – Manhattan distance is calculated as 
𝑑(𝑥,𝑦) = ∣𝑥₁ − 𝑦₁∣ + ∣𝑥₂ − 𝑦₂∣.

𝑧 = (𝑥 − 𝜇)/𝜎.

Answer Key: 
A. True – Euclidean distance between two observations 𝑎and 𝑏is 
𝑑(𝑎,𝑏) = √((𝑋_(1,a) − 𝑋_(1,b))² + (𝑋_(2,a) − 𝑋_(2,b))² + (𝑋_(3,a) − 𝑋_(3,b))²).

cos(𝑎,𝑏) = 𝑎ᵀ𝑏/(∥𝑎∥₂∥𝑏∥₂).
